UX & decision psychology · Miami Spice

The Desire Gap

We won the cognitive half of the decision and left the affective & social half nearly empty. The austerity that makes it a superb tool makes it an incomplete instrument for a choice that is, at heart, about desire and other people.

The scorecard

Two halves of one decision

"Where should we go out?" is hedonic, social, and emotionally loaded. Judged against that job — not against "flight search" — the strengths and gaps sort cleanly.

Won

Cognitive efficiency

Near-exemplary; needs no work.

  • Hick’s Law — the IA is a decision tree
  • Cognitive load — anti-slop = less extraneous load
  • Fitts, Gestalt, chunking, live facet counts
  • No dead ends (relaxations)
Thin

Affective & social

Where every remaining lever lives.

  • Appetite (visceral desire)
  • Trust (social proof)
  • Memory & convergence (shortlist)
  • Commitment (default + group flow)
Behavioralpleasure of use — speed, flow, honesty
Reflectivethe story you tell about the choice
Visceralpre-conscious pull — “that looks delicious”

Every review-heavy competitor is really selling confidence. Reviews are just one way to manufacture it — and not the way users most trust.

The findings

Eight gaps, ranked

By impact × fit-to-domain × constraint-feasibility. Every fix respects the no-reviews, no-per-dish-photos, rows-not-cards, Deco-austerity constraints — and reuses machinery already in the app.

01

No visceral appetite

Norman · visceral / aesthetic-usability limit

Now A monochrome text ledger for an appetite-led choice. The one restaurant image is unused; the most saturated object on screen is the price.

Fix Carry desire in the two channels allowed — language and one image: a real signature-dish callout, the hero image on the detail state, warmth on the food, not only the price.

M
02

Zero on-surface social proof

Cialdini · social proof / Jakob’s Law

Now The top trust lever for going out is legally forced off-surface — and we say nothing, against a Yelp/OpenTable mental model that expects it.

Fix Proof needn’t be their reviews: first-party marks (Michelin, "1 of 8 this year"), a prominent deep-link to Google/Yelp ratings, short editorial one-liners in derived/.

S–M
03

No shortlist / save / compare

Zeigarnik + Miller + group substrate

Now Every candidate you weigh is an open loop held in your head; comparison is "keep three in mind and argue."

Fix A client-side shortlist via localStorage — the exact mechanism already shipped for the AM/PM toggle. Pin, a "Your shortlist (3)" tray, and a shareable URL. No backend, no login.

S–M
04

No confident default

Decision fatigue · defaults / satisficer

Now All tools, no opinion. The flagship list defaults to A–Z — literally no recommendation — for a tired, deferral-prone group.

Fix A "Tonight’s pick / Just pick for us" path staging one defensible choice from data we own (mood fit, choiceScore, tier, tonight’s availability). Maximizers keep every tool; satisficers get a door marked exit.

M
05

The group deadlock is unaddressed

Fogg (B=MAP) + diffusion of responsibility

Now Single-player tool for a multi-player problem. It makes a list to argue over, not a way to converge.

Fix Build convergence on the shortlist: a shareable set, "narrow to 3 then decide," or a neutral "decide for us" that launders the responsibility (why flipping a coin resolves deadlocks).

M
06

Price framed as cost, not value

Von Restorff · anchoring · decoy

Now The one isolated object is the price chip, so users remember spots by price first; "$65" shows with no counter-anchor, reading as expense. A discount event without the discount.

Fix Frame the tier as value — "3 courses, $65" — and keep all three tiers visible (the decoy effect favors the middle). Optionally a second, rare quality isolation.

S
07

No engineered peak; a hand-off ending

Peak-End rule

Now Uniformly calm — which reads as forgettable. The last moment is an outbound link: a departure, not a payoff.

Fix Dramatize the "7 perfect matches" resolution and the mood-lens "found you a gem"; end on a confident confirmation ("Great pick — the one dish to order is ___") before the outbound click.

S–M
08

Honest scarcity left as metadata

Cialdini · scarcity

Now The Aug–Sep window and narrow per-restaurant availability are true and unused — stated as neutral facts, never as motivation.

Fix Non-manipulative urgency: "Restaurant Week ends in N days," and framing genuinely limited availability ("Dinner only, Fri–Sat") as a reason to plan now.

S

Make it warm, trustworthy, decisive
